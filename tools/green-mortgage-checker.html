What green mortgages actually are

A green mortgage is not a different kind of loan. It is a standard residential mortgage with a slightly lower interest rate — typically 0.05 to 0.25 percentage points below the lender's equivalent standard product — offered on homes that meet an energy-efficiency threshold, almost always EPC band A or B. The test case above (£200,000 over 25 years, 4.5% vs 4.35%) saves about £17 a month, or roughly £410 over a two-year fix. That is real money, but it is not life-changing money, and it comes with conditions worth understanding before you let it steer a six-figure borrowing decision.

Why do lenders do this? Partly regulation and partly risk: energy-efficient homes have lower running costs, which means borrowers are less likely to default when energy prices spike — and lenders with green mortgage books can report them against climate targets. Some lenders also offer green additional borrowing: extra funds at preferential rates specifically for retrofit work (insulation, heat pumps, solar), which is arguably the more useful product. A cheaper rate on the main loan is a reward for already being efficient; green additional borrowing is a tool for becoming efficient.

The EPC bar: where most people fall

Here is the catch the adverts whisper: most green mortgages require EPC A or B, and only about 15% of English homes meet that bar. If your home is EPC C or D — where the majority of mortgaged Britain sits — the standard green products are closed to you. A few lenders have stretched to EPC C for certain products, and criteria shift year to year, which is why the calculator asks your band and gives you a straight eligibility read rather than assuming.

This creates a perverse incentive worth naming: the households that would benefit most from cheap retrofit borrowing are the ones locked out of it. If you are EPC D with a plan to reach B, the sequence is: do the fabric work (see the EPC improvement estimator for the cheapest points), get reassessed, then remortgage onto the green rate. Some lenders' green additional-borrowing products are designed exactly for this journey — borrow cheap to fund the improvements that earn the EPC that unlocks the cheap rate.

Key definitions

Green mortgage
A standard mortgage with a discounted rate for energy-efficient homes (usually EPC A/B).
Green additional borrowing
Extra borrowing at preferential rates specifically to fund retrofit work — often more useful than the rate discount itself.
EPC (Energy Performance Certificate)
The A–G efficiency rating required when selling or letting. Valid 10 years; the evidence lenders ask for.
Product transfer
Switching to a new deal with your existing lender — simpler than remortgaging, but you cannot access other lenders' green rates.

Which lenders offer green products (2026 snapshot)

The green mortgage market in 2026 includes most major UK lenders in some form: NatWest, Halifax, Barclays, HSBC, Virgin Money, and several building societies (notably Ecology Building Society, which has been green since before it was fashionable) all offer green products or green additional borrowing. Terms move constantly — discounts appear, disappear, and change size with each rate cycle — so treat any named list, including this one, as a starting point for your own comparison, not a recommendation. The calculator's maths is lender-agnostic: plug in the actual rates you are quoted and compare.

What to ask each lender: the exact EPC requirement (A only? A/B? is C accepted on any product?), whether the EPC must be recent, whether green additional borrowing is available and what work qualifies, and — critically — the fees. A green product with a £999 fee can easily cost more than a standard product with no fee at a slightly higher rate. Compare total cost over the fix period, not headline rate. The calculator shows the rate saving; do the fee arithmetic yourself before signing.

Remortgage vs product transfer

When your fix ends, you have two routes. A product transfer with your current lender is fast and usually fee-light, but you can only choose from that lender's range — if their green discount is thin, you are stuck with thin. A remortgage to a new lender opens the whole market, including the best green rates, but means a new application, valuation, and legal work (often free or subsidised as an incentive). The green-rate difference between lenders is small in absolute terms, so the decision should follow the normal remortgage logic — total cost including fees — with the green discount as a tiebreaker, not the driver. Never pay a large fee to chase a 0.1-point green discount; the calculator will show you it does not wash.

EPC evidence: what lenders actually ask for

Lenders check the EPC register — the certificate must exist, be valid (within 10 years), and show the qualifying band. If your EPC predates improvements, get a new assessment (£60–£120) rather than arguing; assessors can only rate what they can see and evidence. Keep certificates for retrofit work (MCS certificates for heat pumps and solar, FENSA for windows, invoices for insulation) — some lenders' green additional-borrowing products ask for proof the money was spent on qualifying measures, and you will want the paperwork at sale time anyway.

Green mortgages and house prices: does efficiency pay at sale?

The rate discount is only half the story. There is growing evidence that energy-efficient homes command a price premium: government-commissioned research has found EPC improvements associated with price uplifts of a few percent per band in England, with the effect strongest at the bottom of the market (lifting an F/G home to C) and in areas with high energy costs. A 3% premium on a £300,000 home is £9,000 — an order of magnitude larger than any green-mortgage rate saving. This reframes the retrofit maths: insulation and glazing are not just bill-savers but value-adders, and the green mortgage is a small rebate on the journey.

Caveats apply. The premium varies by region and is hard to isolate from correlated factors (efficient homes tend to be newer and better maintained). And valuers remain conservative — many still give efficiency little weight in formal valuations, which matters if you are borrowing against the improved value. But the direction of travel is clear: as EPCs become more prominent in listings and minimum standards tighten for rentals, efficiency is being priced in. The green mortgage discount is the market's small, early signal of a much larger repricing to come. Buyers already ask about bills; soon they will discount draughty homes the way they currently discount short leases.

The honest verdict on green mortgages

Green mortgages are a genuine but modest benefit: a small discount for homes that are already efficient, plus — more valuably — cheap borrowing to fund the journey there. They are not a reason to overpay for an A-rated new build, not a substitute for comparing total deal costs, and not available to most of the homes that need retrofit most. Use the calculator to size the saving honestly, chase the discount if you qualify, and if you do not qualify yet, treat the EPC threshold as a target: the whole-house retrofit planner will sequence the work that gets you there. The cheapest mortgage is still, overwhelmingly, the cheapest total deal — green is the garnish, not the meal.

Frequently asked questions

How much cheaper is a green mortgage?

Typically 0.05-0.25 percentage points below the lender's standard equivalent rate. On £200,000 over 25 years, a 0.15-point discount saves ~£17/month — around £410 over a two-year fix. Always compare total cost including fees.

What EPC rating do I need for a green mortgage?

Most require EPC A or B; a few products accept C. D and below do not qualify. Only ~15% of English homes are A/B, so check your EPC on the national register before applying.

Can I get a green mortgage to fund insulation or a heat pump?

Yes — several lenders offer green additional borrowing: extra funds at preferential rates specifically for retrofit work. This is often more useful than the small rate discount on the main loan.

Is it worth remortgaging just for a green rate?

Rarely on the rate alone — the saving is small. Remortgage on normal total-cost logic (rate + fees across the market) and treat the green discount as a tiebreaker. Never pay a large fee to chase 0.1 points.

Does the EPC have to be recent?

It must be valid (issued within 10 years) and on the national register showing the qualifying band. If you have improved the home since the last EPC, get reassessed (£60-£120) before applying.

Which UK lenders offer green mortgages?

Most majors offer something: NatWest, Halifax, Barclays, HSBC, Virgin Money and building societies including Ecology. Terms change constantly — compare live deals rather than relying on any list.
