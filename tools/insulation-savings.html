Why insulation beats almost everything else

Every discussion of home retrofit eventually arrives at the same unglamorous conclusion: insulation first. The physics is unforgiving — in a typical uninsulated UK home, around a third of heat escapes through the walls, a quarter through the roof, and the rest through floors, windows and draughts. No heating system, however efficient, can economically overcome a building that leaks warmth as fast as it is made. Insulation is also the only retrofit measure that reduces the size of everything that follows: an insulated house needs a smaller, cheaper heat pump, fewer solar panels to cover its demand, and lower-capacity radiators. That is why every credible retrofit framework — from the Energy Saving Trust to PAS 2035, the UK's retrofit quality standard — sequences fabric before systems.

This calculator scales Energy Saving Trust typical savings by property type, because a detached house has far more exposed wall and roof area than a flat. The baseline figures assume a detached house; the semi factor of 0.7, terrace 0.6 and flat 0.4 reflect the reduced external surface. These are planning figures, not promises — your actual saving depends on your current state (going from nothing to 270 mm of loft insulation saves far more than topping up from 150 mm) and on your energy tariff. The five measures, honestly ranked

Loft insulation is the undisputed champion of cost-effectiveness. Rolling mineral wool to 270 mm in an empty loft costs £300–£600, saves a detached house around £300 a year, and pays back in under two years. It is also the most DIY-able measure on this site — competent DIYers can do it in a weekend, though the hatch, pipework and downlights need proper treatment (downlights need fire-safe covers, not insulation piled on top). The main caveat is boarding: if you use the loft for storage, raised boarding on stilts preserves the insulation's depth, because compressed insulation loses performance.

Cavity wall insulation fills the gap in cavity walls with mineral wool, beads or foam, typically in half a day, for £500–£1,000. The £350/year detached saving makes it the second-best value. But — and this is a genuine but — it is not suitable for every cavity. Homes in severe wind-driven rain exposure, with faulty wall ties, existing damp, or narrow cavities can suffer badly from fill that bridges moisture across. Our cavity wall suitability checker exists precisely because this decision is safety-critical; never let a salesperson talk you past damp or exposure concerns.

Solid wall insulation is the big one for pre-1920s Britain. Internal wall insulation (£5,000–£12,000 for a typical house) loses you 50–100 mm of room depth per treated wall but is cheaper and needs no scaffolding; external wall insulation (£8,000–£18,000) wraps the house in a weatherproof jacket, transforms the appearance, and avoids disturbing interiors, but needs planning consideration in conservation areas. Both save around £500 a year on a detached house — yet even so, the payback stretches past a decade on bills alone. The real case for solid wall insulation is comfort (warm walls end condensation and cold spots), EPC bands, and property value; judge it on those, not the payback figure above.

Floor insulation addresses the draughty suspended timber floors of Victorian and Edwardian houses. Insulation fitted between joists from below (or from above during a re-floor) saves around £150 a year for a detached house and — crucially — eliminates the cold draughts that make rooms feel chilly even when the thermostat says 20°C. It is disruptive if done from above (furniture out, floorboards up), so the smart time is during any planned flooring work. Solid concrete floors are much harder to treat and usually not worth it.

Key definitions

270 mm loft standard
The recommended depth for mineral wool loft insulation in the UK — roughly the depth of two rolls.
Cavity wall
Two wall leaves with a gap between, standard in UK homes built roughly 1920–1990. The gap can be filled with insulation.
Solid wall
A single-thickness wall (usually 9-inch brick) in pre-1920s homes — no cavity to fill, so insulation goes inside or outside.
U-value
Heat loss rate through a material. An uninsulated solid wall is ~2.1 W/m²K; with external insulation, ~0.3.
PAS 2035
The UK standard for whole-house retrofit: assessment, design and monitoring by qualified coordinators.

Comfort is the hidden return

The payback figures above understate insulation's value because they only count bill savings. Ask anyone who has insulated a cold house and they will talk about comfort first: rooms that hold temperature overnight, walls you can sit against without flinching, the end of condensation streaming down bedroom windows on winter mornings. Condensation matters beyond comfort — persistent damp feeds mould, and mould aggravates asthma and respiratory conditions, which is one reason fuel-poverty researchers treat insulation as a health intervention as much as an energy one. If anyone in your household has asthma, the case for wall and loft insulation is medical as well as financial.

Grants that pay for insulation

Before paying full price, check whether someone else will pay. ECO4 obliges energy suppliers to fund insulation and heating upgrades for low-income and vulnerable households — fully funded, including solid wall insulation that would otherwise cost five figures. The Great British Insulation Scheme covers a single insulation measure for homes in council tax bands A–D with EPC D–G in England. Home Energy Scotland offers up to £7,500 in grants plus interest-free loans. And every UK installation of insulation currently benefits from 0% VAT. Our grant and subsidy finder matches your circumstances to the live schemes in under a minute — run it before you get quotes, because grant-funded work must use approved installers and the order matters.

Installer quality: the difference between saving and disaster

Insulation is simple physics but fiddly craft, and bad installation ranges from useless to destructive. Cavity fill in an unsuitable wall can cause penetrating damp; loft insulation stuffed into eaves blocks ventilation and rots roof timbers; internal wall insulation without a vapour strategy moves the dew point into the wall and grows mould behind the plasterboard. The protections are straightforward: use a TrustMark-registered installer, insist on a guarantee (CIGA for cavity wall, for example), and for anything beyond loft top-up consider PAS 2035 assessment — a retrofit coordinator spots the moisture risks a salesperson will not mention. The cheapest quote for wall insulation is very often the most expensive mistake.

What insulation cannot do

Insulation slows heat loss; it does not create heat, fix a broken boiler, or ventilate a house. Two cautions follow from that. First, as you seal a home up, ventilation matters more, not less — extractor fans, trickle vents and occasional airing prevent the stuffy, moisture-laden air that insulation can trap. Never draught-proof or insulate your way past the need for controlled ventilation. Second, insulation will not rescue a home with fundamental damp: fix leaking gutters, failed pointing and rising damp before insulating, or you will simply be insulating a wet wall more expensively.

Insulation and your EPC band

Each measure here moves your SAP score: loft to 270 mm is worth roughly +8 points, cavity fill +10, solid wall insulation +15. Two measures can lift a band-D home into band C — the threshold that increasingly matters for green mortgages and proposed rental standards. Frequently asked questions

Which insulation should I do first?

Loft insulation to 270 mm — it is the cheapest major measure (£300–£600), pays back in under two years, and is often DIY-able. Next, cavity wall fill if your walls are suitable (check with our suitability checker). Solid wall insulation comes last on pure payback but first on comfort.

How much does cavity wall insulation cost in the UK?

Typically £500–£1,000 for a whole house, installed in half a day. It is one of the cheapest professional measures, but only suitable for walls without damp, with adequate cavity width, and not in severe wind-driven rain exposure.

Is solid wall insulation worth it?

On bill savings alone the payback is long (10–20+ years at £8,000–£18,000 cost). It is worth it for comfort, condensation control, EPC improvement and property value — and it may be fully funded under ECO4 if you are eligible. Judge it on the full picture, not just the payback.

Can I install loft insulation myself?

Yes — it is the most DIY-friendly retrofit measure. Use 270 mm of mineral wool, leave eaves ventilation clear, fit fire-safe covers over downlights, and use raised boarding on stilts if you need storage. Wear a mask and gloves; the fibres are unpleasant.

Will insulation cause damp or condensation?

Done badly, yes — which is why installer quality matters. Cavity fill in unsuitable walls, blocked eaves ventilation, and internal wall insulation without moisture planning can all cause damp. Use TrustMark-registered installers and guarantees, and get a suitability check for cavity walls.

Does insulation work in summer too?

Yes — insulation slows heat flow in both directions, so a well-insulated loft keeps the house cooler in heatwaves as well as warmer in winter. It is one of the cheapest passive cooling measures available.
